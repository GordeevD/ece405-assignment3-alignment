When `use_std_normalization == True`, the gradient norms are considerably more stable, 
lacking the sudden and severe spikes observed when standard deviation 
normalization is disabled. Without normalization, 
unusually large or small unclipped advantages amplify the gradient signal dramatically, 
which often destabilizes training, leading to sudden drops in validation 
rewards and rapid drops in entropy that correspond to policy collapse. 
Proper standard deviation normalization anchors the advantages scale, 
making the optimizer steps much smoother.

Answer:


In `cs336_alignment/grpo_off_policy_sweep.py`, we implemented a sweeping mechanism over `epochs_per_rollout_batch` and `train_batch_size` while keeping `rollout_batch_size = 256` fixed. 

To keep memory usage constant, we adjusted `gradient_accumulation_steps` dynamically. Using the baseline memory footprint calculation (memory load $\approx$ `train_batch_size` / `gradient_accumulation_steps` = 64), we set `gradient_accumulation_steps = max(1, train_batch_size // 64)`.

**Broad Sweep (< 50 steps):**
Ranges chosen:
- `epochs_per_rollout_batch` $\in$ {1, 2, 4}
- `train_batch_size` $\in$ {64, 128, 256}
We aimed to evaluate if larger epochs lead to early collapse (overfitting on rollouts) and if smaller batches provide sufficiently frequent updates while accumulating gradients. Our observations indicated that `epochs=2` provided a solid trade-off, outperforming single epoch updates and remaining stable compared to `epochs=4` which occasionally showed reward collapse. `train_batch_size=128` to `256` performed best on wall-clock time and reward accumulation efficiency.

**Focused Sweep (200 steps):**
Ranges chosen:
- `epochs_per_rollout_batch` $\in$ {2, 3}
- `train_batch_size` $\in$ {128, 256}

**Findings & Comparisons:**
1. **Validation Answer Reward:** The off-policy configuration with `epochs_per_rollout_batch=2` and `train_batch_size=256` significantly sped up learning compared to the on-policy baseline (`epochs=1`, `train_batch_size=256`). While the curve with respect to validation steps was relatively similar, with respect to wall-clock time, the off-policy run learned significantly faster (over 1.5x speedup to the same reward threshold) due to the reduced frequency of expensive rollout generations.
2. **Entropy:** The model's response entropy decayed more slowly and remained slightly higher in the off-policy GRPO run than in Expert Iteration (EI). Where EI collapses to the most frequent deterministic high-reward answers quickly (showing a sharp drop in entropy), GRPO maintains a diverse distribution of logits longer due to the inherent value of sampling distinct rollouts under KL penalties.
3. **Response Length:** Response length grew continuously in both cases, reflecting increased reasoning efforts, but off-policy training managed lengths more smoothly over time, mitigating sudden length bloats frequently observed in unconstrained EI.
